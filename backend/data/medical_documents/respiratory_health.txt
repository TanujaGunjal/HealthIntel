RESPIRATORY HEALTH INFORMATION
Source: General Health Reference (Educational Use)

COMMON COLD
The common cold is a viral infection of the upper respiratory tract. It is one of the most frequent illnesses experienced by people worldwide. The common cold is caused by rhinoviruses in most cases, though other viruses can also cause it.

Symptoms typically include:
- Runny or stuffy nose
- Sore throat
- Cough
- Congestion
- Slight body aches or mild headache
- Sneezing
- Low-grade fever
- Generally feeling unwell

Most colds last 7 to 10 days. Symptoms are often worst during the first two to three days. Rest and staying hydrated are important for recovery. Over-the-counter medications may help relieve symptoms. Antibiotics are not effective against viral infections.

INFLUENZA (FLU)
Influenza is a contagious respiratory illness caused by influenza viruses. It can cause mild to severe illness. Serious outcomes can result in hospitalisation or death. Older people, young children, and people with certain health conditions are at higher risk.

Flu symptoms include:
- Fever or feeling feverish and having chills
- Cough
- Sore throat
- Runny or stuffy nose
- Muscle or body aches
- Headaches
- Fatigue
- Some people may have vomiting and diarrhoea

Unlike the common cold, flu usually appears suddenly. People with flu often feel some or all of these symptoms.

PNEUMONIA
Pneumonia is an infection that inflames the air sacs in one or both lungs. The air sacs may fill with fluid or pus, causing symptoms such as a cough with phlegm or pus, fever, chills, and difficulty breathing. A variety of organisms — bacteria, viruses and fungi — can cause pneumonia.

Symptoms include:
- Chest pain when breathing or coughing
- Confusion or changes in mental awareness in adults
- Cough, which may produce phlegm
- Fatigue
- Fever, sweating and shaking chills
- Lower than normal body temperature in older adults
- Nausea, vomiting or diarrhoea
- Shortness of breath

Medical evaluation and treatment are important for pneumonia as it can be serious.

ASTHMA
Asthma is a condition in which airways narrow and swell, and may produce extra mucus. This can make breathing difficult and trigger coughing, a whistling sound (wheezing) when exhaling, and shortness of breath. For some people, asthma is a minor nuisance. For others, it can be a major problem that interferes with daily activities.

Key symptoms:
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness or pain
- Wheezing when exhaling
- Trouble sleeping caused by shortness of breath, coughing or wheezing
- Coughing or wheezing attacks that are worsened by a respiratory virus such as a cold or the flu

Asthma triggers vary by person and can include airborne allergens, exercise, respiratory infections, cold air, and air pollutants.

BRONCHITIS
Bronchitis is an inflammation of the lining of the bronchial tubes, which carry air to and from the lungs. People who have bronchitis often cough up thickened mucus, which can be discoloured.

Acute bronchitis symptoms include:
- Cough
- Production of mucus (sputum)
- Fatigue
- Shortness of breath
- Slight fever and chills
- Chest discomfort

Acute bronchitis usually develops from a cold or other respiratory infection. Chronic bronchitis is a more serious condition that develops over time.

ALLERGIC RHINITIS (HAY FEVER)
Allergic rhinitis is an allergic response to specific allergens such as pollen, dust mites, or pet dander. When someone with allergic rhinitis breathes in allergens, their immune system overreacts.

Symptoms include:
- Runny nose and nasal congestion
- Watery, itchy, red eyes
- Sneezing
- Itchy nose, roof of mouth or throat
- Swollen, blue-coloured skin under the eyes
- Postnasal drip
- Cough
- Decreased sense of smell
