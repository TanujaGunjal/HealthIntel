DERMATOLOGICAL AND GENERAL HEALTH INFORMATION
Source: General Health Reference (Educational Use)

ECZEMA (ATOPIC DERMATITIS)
Eczema is a condition that makes the skin red and itchy. It is common in children but can occur at any age. Atopic dermatitis is long-lasting (chronic) and tends to flare periodically.

Symptoms include:
- Dry skin
- Itching, which may be severe, especially at night
- Red or brownish-grey patches on the skin
- Small, raised bumps that may weep fluid when scratched
- Thickened, cracked, scaly skin
- Raw, swollen skin from scratching

PSORIASIS
Psoriasis is a skin disease that causes a rash with itchy, scaly patches, most commonly on the knees, elbows, trunk and scalp. Psoriasis is a common, long-term (chronic) disease with no cure.

Symptoms include:
- Red patches of skin covered with thick, silvery scales
- Dry, cracked skin that may bleed or itch
- Itching, burning or soreness
- Thickened, pitted or ridged nails
- Swollen and stiff joints

HIVES (URTICARIA)
Hives are a skin reaction that causes itchy welts (wheals) that range in size from small spots to large blotches.

Signs and symptoms include:
- Batches of red or skin-coloured welts on the skin
- Welts that vary in size
- Itching, which may be severe
- Painful swelling (angioedema) of the lips, eyelids and inside the throat
- Tendency to flare with triggers such as heat, exercise or stress

DIABETES (GENERAL INFORMATION)
Diabetes is a group of diseases that result in too much sugar in the blood (high blood glucose). The two main types are type 1 and type 2 diabetes.

Common symptoms:
- Increased thirst
- Frequent urination
- Extreme hunger
- Unexplained weight loss
- Presence of ketones in the urine
- Fatigue
- Irritability
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing sores
- Frequent infections

Type 2 diabetes symptoms may be mild and develop slowly. Some people have no symptoms initially.

HIGH BLOOD PRESSURE (HYPERTENSION)
High blood pressure (hypertension) is a common condition in which the long-term force of the blood against the artery walls is high enough that it may eventually cause health problems.

Most people with high blood pressure have no signs or symptoms, even if readings reach dangerously high levels. A few people with high blood pressure may have:
- Headaches
- Shortness of breath
- Nosebleeds

These signs and symptoms typically don't occur until high blood pressure has reached a severe or life-threatening stage.

ANAEMIA
Anaemia is a condition in which a person lacks enough healthy red blood cells to carry adequate oxygen to the body's tissues.

Symptoms vary depending on the cause of the anaemia, and may include:
- Fatigue
- Weakness
- Pale or yellowish skin
- Irregular heartbeats
- Shortness of breath
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Chest pain
- Cold hands and feet
- Headaches

FEVER
Fever is a temporary increase in body temperature, often due to an illness. A temperature above 38°C (100.4°F) is generally considered a fever in adults.

Associated symptoms:
- Sweating
- Chills and shivering
- Headache
- Muscle aches
- Loss of appetite
- Irritability
- Dehydration
- General weakness

Fever is usually a sign that the body is fighting an infection. Most fevers resolve on their own with rest and adequate fluids. Seek medical attention for very high fever, fever in infants, or fever with severe symptoms.

DEHYDRATION
Dehydration occurs when the body uses or loses more fluid than it takes in, and the body doesn't have enough water and other fluids to carry out its normal functions.

Symptoms in adults:
- Extreme thirst
- Less frequent urination
- Dark-coloured urine
- Fatigue
- Dizziness
- Confusion
